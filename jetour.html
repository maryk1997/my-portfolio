JETOUR Georgia

A static event invitation became a working brand touchpoint: a responsive, bilingual digital product with RSVP capture, a live guest list and its own testing pass, built for a real client deadline rather than a portfolio brief.

Role
Product Concept, Creative Direction, Front-End, QA
Client
Commercial project, brand event, Georgia
Languages
Georgian / English (bilingual UX)
Stack
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Google Apps Script, Google Sheets
View Live View Code
THE CONTEXT

An invitation that needed to do more than announce a date.

The brief started as a request for a digital invitation for a brand event. But a one-off static page couldn't answer the questions the client actually had: who is confirmed, who still needs a reminder, and how do guests get the practical details of location, schedule and language without a phone call.

I reframed the deliverable from "a page" to "a small product": something the client could point people to, that would quietly collect the data they needed, and that would work the same whether a guest opened it in Georgian or English.

THE APPROACH

Design, build and data as one workflow.

I owned the project end to end: art direction and layout, the front-end build, the RSVP logic, and the QA pass before handoff. Because I was designer, developer and tester on the same project, I could make trade-offs quickly. I simplified the RSVP form the moment I noticed, while testing on my own phone, that a multi-step flow was friction guests wouldn't tolerate on an invitation.

Bilingual UX

Georgian and English content structured from the same layout rather than bolted on afterward, so the type-setting and rhythm hold up in both scripts.

RSVP Google Sheets

A lightweight RSVP form posts through Google Apps Script into a live Sheet, giving the client a real-time guest list with no backend to maintain.

Countdown & logistics

A live countdown, event schedule and location details, built to stay legible and fast on mobile, where most guests actually opened the link.

QA

Treated like a product, not a one-off page.

Before handoff I ran a manual QA pass across breakpoints and both languages: form validation and submission edge cases, broken states on slow connections, and cross-checking that every Sheet entry matched what was submitted in the browser. That habit of building something and then trying to break it comes directly from the QA training I did alongside front-end development.

OUTCOME

Shipped, used, and extended.

The site went live and ran the client's guest communication and RSVP collection for the event. The same approach was later reused for a second brand activation, JETOUR / II, which is the clearest proof that the first build held up well enough to repeat.
