Casa Gelso

A luxury fashion house opens in Tbilisi on 12 September 2026, carrying Ferragamo, Moschino, Tom Ford and Bottega Veneta. It had a name, a date and an address. I built everything that came between those facts and the guest standing in the room: the campaign idea, the story, the words in three languages, the art direction, the interface, the code and the testing.

Role
Sole author. Concept, campaign, copy, design, front-end, testing

Scope
Three-act launch experience: teaser digital ad private invitation
Languages

THE BRIEF

A shop opening is not news. It has to be made into a feeling.

Tbilisi already has places to buy designer clothing. So "a new store is opening" is not a reason for anyone to care, and a page with a logo and a date would have been an announcement, not a launch. The real problem was one of desire: how do you make people want to be in a room they have never seen, for a brand they have never heard of, four weeks before it exists?

I decided the answer was to withhold. The campaign would show the clothes before it showed the name, show the name before it explained anything, and only ask for something, an RSVP, once it had earned the right to. Three acts, one continuous scroll, no page reloads between them.

ACT ONE / TEASER

Five frames that explain nothing.

The experience opens on a strappy heel on a pedestal edge, then a hand reaching for a heart-shaped bag, then a black halter gown. Each frame carries three words and nothing else: something is coming, something to love, something to desire, make it yours, wear the moment. No brand name, no address, no price. The only fixed point is the date, 12/09/2026, sitting in the corner the whole time.

I wrote the captions as a single sentence broken across five images so a viewer has to keep going to finish the thought. There is a skip control from the first second, because forcing an intro on someone is the fastest way to lose them.

ACT TWO / DIGITAL AD

The brand reveal, released one chapter at a time.

The second act is the advertisement, and it unfolds as you scroll: five numbered chapters, each pinned to a full-bleed image, each delivering exactly one idea. The fashion you dreamed of, now in your city. Shop Tbilisi, feel Milan. Iconic names, one destination. Closer than it has ever been. Come and see it in person.

This is the act that does the commercial work. It is where Ferragamo, Moschino, Tom Ford and Bottega Veneta finally appear, running as a marquee under the promise. The line "shop Tbilisi, feel Milan" is the whole positioning of the business compressed into four words, and it is the sentence the client now uses to describe themselves.

ACT THREE / INVITATION

Only now does it ask for something.

The final act turns the campaign into a private invitation: date, time, the address on Kostava Street linked straight into Google Maps, and an add-to-calendar action so the event survives being closed in a browser tab. Then the RSVP.

  • Name, and a yes or no attendance choice, the two fields the client actually needs.
  • A question asking each guest for their top three fashion houses, which quietly hands the buyer real demand data before the doors open.
  • An open field asking "what do you wish for Casa Gelso?", which turns a form into a guest book.
  • A hidden honeypot field to absorb bot submissions without putting a captcha in front of a luxury guest.
DESIGN DECISIONS

Restraint is the luxury signal.

The palette is a single deep wine against ivory, with a mulberry monogram that I drew and set as the only recurring mark. There is no gold gradient, no drop shadow, no second accent colour. Luxury retail communicates status through what it refuses to do, so the discipline of the design system is itself part of the argument.

There is a score, because the campaign is built to be experienced with sound, but it never plays without a visible mute control that stays reachable, and the site is complete in silence. Roman numerals date the collection, MMXXVI, because the house is presenting itself as something older than its opening date.

Three languages

English, Georgian and Italian, switchable anywhere in the journey without losing your place. I wrote and translated all three. Italian is there because the brands are Italian, and the borrowed authority matters.

Sound, optional

An audio bed that sets the tempo of the teaser, with mute exposed in the navigation from the first frame rather than buried.

Continuous scroll

Three acts on one page, so the campaign never breaks the spell with a page load, and so a single shared link contains the whole story.

Built to be shared

Full Open Graph and Twitter card metadata with a chosen preview frame, because in practice this campaign lives as a link pasted into a message thread.

TECHNICAL

Hand-built, no framework, no page builder.

The whole experience is HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript, deployed on a custom domain. That was a decision, not a limitation: a launch campaign is read on a phone, often on mobile data, often for under a minute, and every kilobyte of framework I didn't ship is a person who didn't leave while waiting.

Structure
Semantic HTML with a skip-to-content link and labelled required fields.
Motion
Scroll-driven chapter reveals and a timed intro sequence with a skip control.
Localisation
EN / KA / IT switching, with Georgian and Latin type set to hold the same rhythm.
Data
RSVP capture including attendance, brand preference and a free-text field, with a honeypot spam trap.
Integrations
Google Maps deep link and add-to-calendar for the opening.
Distribution
Canonical URL, Open Graph, Twitter card and theme colour, on a custom domain.
QA

Then I tried to break it.

A trilingual site with audio, an intro sequence and a form has a lot of ways to fail quietly. I ran a manual pass across breakpoints and all three languages: whether Georgian text still fits when it runs a third longer than English, whether the audio ever starts unmuted, whether the intro can be skipped mid-animation, whether the form rejects an empty required field and accepts a valid one, and what happens on a slow connection before the images arrive.

Doing my own QA is a habit from the manual testing training I took after learning to build, and it is the reason I trust the work enough to put my client's opening night on it.
